## The rule, and the exceptions, in the user's own words

Found in the source rather than reconstructed — `Net/Board/BoardTunePages.cs` and
`Net/Remote/RemoteBoardContent.cs` both carry it verbatim:

> "Die 1:1 Regel besagt dass alle remote Spieler immer 1:1 das am Board (mit den expliziten
> ausgemachten Ausnahmen) sieht. Ändert ein Spieler also die Positionen für sich selber, so sollen
> alle anderen diese Position bei seinem board auch sehen." — 2026-08-09
>
> "generell gilt die Regel, das man alle **Interaktionen, Animationen und Anzeigen** des
> Controllboards in MP auch synchronisieren soll. **Die einzige Ausnahme ist hier die geheime Quest
> des characters und während der Auswahlphase die tatsächlichen Oberseiten der Karten.**" — 2026-08-08

So the scope is the **control board**, and the agreed exceptions are exactly two.

## Finding 1 — the board's own dimensions were written three times, unguarded · **FIXED**

`BoardW = 0.64f` / `BoardH = 0.32f` are declared **three times**: `Cards/Tray/PlayTray.6.Build.cs`,
`Net/Remote/RemoteControlBoard.cs`, `Net/Remote/RemoteBoardFurniture.cs`. Nothing tied them
together.

The intent was never in doubt — `RemoteControlBoard` states it at its own declaration: *"in the
same 'card real-metre' units as the local board (PlayTray BoardW/H), so scaling by the owner's
BoardScale reproduces their board's world size."*

**Why it matters more than any dial in the census:** this is the size of the object the whole rule
is about. A retune of the local board would have left both mirrors at the old size and every peer
would have seen a differently-sized board — and it is invisible from inside the tuner's own
headset, which is how every one of these has been found.

Two `check-mirrors.sh` groups added; verified red by changing one copy to `0.65f` (the lint fails
and names all three sites). 18 → 20 groups.

## Finding 2 — decision prompts other than take-damage are mirrored in WORDING, not in APPEARANCE · **CLOSED, and this finding's ATTRIBUTION was wrong**

> **PARTLY CORRECTED, 2026-08-27** — see `DESIGN-1TO1-RESIDUE.md` §1. This section originally cited
> item decisions as the case. That attribution was **wrong**: the reported "nur eine box" was a
> mirrored USE-BAR SLOT, and `RemoteUseBarSymbols` retired it with **zero wire bytes** by resolving
> the game's own icon on the receiving machine. What genuinely remains under `DecisionRoleUnknown`
> is two prompts and only two: the short-rest `YesNoDialog` and `DialogPopup`'s option buttons.


The wire defines four decision roles: `Unknown`, `BurnAvailable`, `BurnDiscarded`, `TakeDamage`
(`DecisionRoleMax = 3`). Anything the sampler cannot attribute to a known game widget — **an item
decision, the short-rest Yes/No, a `DialogPopup` option** — travels as `DecisionRoleUnknown`, and
`NetProtocol` says what the receiver then draws:

> "The receiver falls back to the mod-drawn plate with record 12's wording — **the pre-record
> look**."

So the peer reads the right words on a plate, and the owner sees the game's own widget. Under
"alle **Anzeigen** des Controllboards" that is a divergence, and item decisions are the case the
user named by name.

**The code states why, and the reason is real rather than an oversight:** the take-damage panel is
a per-client `Singleton` the game populates on *every* machine, so a receiver owns the very widgets
it is asked to mirror. The short-rest `YesNoDialog` belongs to a HAND, and `DialogPopup`'s option
buttons are POOLED and labelled at runtime — neither exists on a peer in the state the owner is
looking at. `NetProtocol` also refuses to reserve role ids for them, citing the project's own
scar: *"a role a receiver does not consume is a wire field with no consumer, which is the failure
this project has already shipped once."*

**Not fixed here.** Closing it means giving the receiver a way to reconstruct those widgets, which
is a feature with a wire design, not a hygiene edit. It is recorded so it is a decision rather
than a surprise.

## Finding 3 — seven of the fourteen wire debts are ANIMATIONS, which the ruling names explicitly · **CLOSED**

`check-wire-coverage.py` prints 14 PENDING debts. The ruling covers "Interaktionen, **Animationen**
und Anzeigen", and seven of the fourteen are animations of the control board:

| dial | what a peer does not see |
|---|---|
| `[ButtonAnim] Enable` | whether the owner's caps animate at all |
| `[ButtonAnim] AppearSeconds` / `DisappearSeconds` | the crumble/assemble clock — a pair of **static consts** on `RemoteBoardFurniture` shared by every peer's board, not per-peer state |
| `[ButtonAnim] AppearParticles` | the spark burst |
| `[Cards] CardDust` | the dust burst |
| `[Cards] GameCardParticles` | card smoke |
| `[Cards] FanCloseDuration` | the fan collapse — **id 156 is declared and deliberately not sampled**, because `RemoteHandFan` has no collapse animation to drive; sampling it would put bytes on the wire no receiver reads and let the checker call it covered |

`FanCloseDuration`'s note is the honest one to keep in view: the debt is a **renderer** debt, not a
wire debt, and wiring it first would produce a green checker and an unchanged picture.

The remaining seven debts are geometry: three `ActiveGridSpacing_*` (record 28 has no 2-component
kind), three `ItemCardOffset_*` (half covered — the open item fan's anchor rides record 5, the HELD
item card has no offset path), and `SlotCardInset` (nothing in `Net/` reads it yet).

## What the audit checked and found correct

- **No control-board display is missing a mirror object.** Every named local mount —
  `ActiveMount`, `DecisionMount`, `ElementMount`, `InitiativeMount`, `ObjectivesMount`, `PileMount`,
  `RoundReadout`, `PickStatusBanner`, `ItemUseSlot`, the rest tokens, the caps, the handle — has a
  counterpart on the remote side.
- **The divergence the user reported for the decision area is fixed by construction, not by
  agreement.** `RemoteDecisionWidgets` reads the owner's own symbols — `PlayTray.DecisionMountWidth`,
  `PlayTray.DecisionMountMaxHeight`, `DecisionDockSurface.DensityScale` — and says why: *"which
  makes the two identical by construction rather than by two formulas agreeing."* It also carries
  its own scale node because the owner's dock lets the mount's `lossyScale` carry the dock scale;
  reproducing that with a bare board-local mount would have rendered the mirrored row at **62 %** of
  the owner's size at the shipped 1.6×.
- **The two agreed exceptions are implemented as exceptions, not as gaps.** The initiative number
  and initiative FX of a foreign player during `SelectAbilityCardsOrLongRest` are deliberately not
  mirrored, and the mirror keeps *this* client's entitled view — the strictly less-informed one —
  rather than carrying the owner's.

## The criterion this audit used, for the next one

A mirror is 1:1-safe when it **reads the local side's own symbol**, and at risk when it holds its
own copy of a number. That criterion came from the code that got it right, and it is what turned
up Finding 1. A census of numeric members in `Net/` that are neither pinned by
`check-remote-defaults.py` nor read from the local side returns **409 in 43 files** — mostly
transport parameters, which are not 1:1 surface. Narrowing that census to members whose name or use
is geometric is the next audit's starting point, and would be worth turning into a checker.

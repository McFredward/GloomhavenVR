render targets inside the head's pre-render callback. Restoring
`RenderTexture.active` does not restore a partial viewport. A small actual GPU
probe establishes that target/viewport distinction; its ordinary mono camera
still resets state before drawing correctly. It cannot reproduce or establish
the OpenXR headset defect.

On Quest, finish these ordinary-target operations at head pre-cull, after the
lower-depth source cameras and before late stereo draw setup. Keep per-eye
texture selection at pre-render. Desktop retains its existing timing. Defer
consumer Debug readbacks until the next head pre-cull and explicitly label their
previous-pass observation. Preserve the original decoder, clock, audio, alpha,
near/far semantics, independent UI glass and both stereo depth images. No new
XR target binding, projection setter, pose adjustment or quality override.

Unity documents both relevant boundaries:
[target changes reset the viewport](https://docs.unity3d.com/2021.3/Documentation/ScriptReference/Rendering.CommandBuffer.SetViewport.html)
and [the pre-render callback precedes render-target setup](https://docs.unity3d.com/2021.3/Documentation/ScriptReference/Camera-onPreRender.html).
Consequently the GPU probe establishes a state hazard, not the headset cause.
The next hardware run must establish whether moving it earlier resolves either
reported symptom.

sample lifetime bound. Record actual provider target descriptors, viewport,
